How thin is the crust, really?

If Earth were the size of an apple, the crust we all live on would be thinner than the apple's skin! It's only about 5 km thick under the oceans and around 30 km thick under the continents. That's tiny compared to Earth's 12,750 km width.

The ground is a giant puzzle!

Earth's shell is cracked into giant pieces called plates. There are 7 major ones! Tap a piece to learn its real name.

The pieces float on hot goo!

Under the plates, hot rock slowly rises, spreads out, cools, and sinks, like soup bubbling on a stove. This is called a convection current, and it's what pushes the puzzle pieces around!

The plates move about 2–5 centimeters a year, roughly as fast as your fingernails grow. Slower than a snail, but over millions of years, it adds up to whole oceans opening and closing!

What happens when pieces meet? Tap to find out!

Bump! (convergent)

Tap me!
When two pieces crash together, the ground pushes UP and makes tall mountains! That's exactly how the Himalayas, Earth's tallest mountains, are still growing today.

Slide! (transform)

Tap me!
When two pieces grind past each other, the ground can suddenly SHAKE. That's an earthquake! California's San Andreas Fault is a famous example, running right through the state.

Pull apart! (divergent)

Tap me!
When two pieces pull apart, hot melty rock called magma rises up and hardens into brand new ground! This is happening right now under the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. It's how Iceland keeps growing.

Fire mountains! Tap the volcano!

Super hot melted rock called magma collects in a chamber underground and sneaks up through a crack. When it bursts out, that's a volcano erupting!

Tap the mountain!

Not all volcanoes look alike! Tap to compare!

Shield volcano

Tap me!
Wide and gently sloped, built by runny lava that flows far before hardening. Hawaii's volcanoes are shield volcanoes. Some rise higher than Mount Everest, measured from the ocean floor!

Stratovolcano

Tap me!
Tall and steep, built from layers of hardened lava and ash stacked over thousands of eruptions. Japan's Mount Fuji is a famous stratovolcano.

Cinder cone

Tap me!
Small and steep, made of loose volcanic rock. Mexico's Parícutin volcano burst out of a farmer's cornfield in 1943 and grew 400 meters tall in just one year!

The Ring of Fire

Most of Earth's volcanoes circle the edge of the Pacific Ocean, where plates are crashing and sliding together. People call this the Ring of Fire.

About 75% of all the world's volcanoes, more than 450 of them, are found along this one ring, and most of Earth's earthquakes happen there too!

Long, long ago... one BIG land!

Did you know? Tap a card!

Everest is still growing
India keeps crashing into Asia, so the Himalayas, and Mount Everest, grow about 4mm taller every year!
Hawaii is on the move
Hawaii's islands formed one by one as the Pacific Plate slowly slid over a hot spot, like a conveyor belt over a candle flame.
Iceland is splitting apart
Iceland sits right on top of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. It's slowly being pulled in two directions as new crust forms beneath it!
Dino world was different
Dinosaurs never saw today's map. The continents looked totally different back then!
